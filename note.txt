==============================================================================
  1  THE SHORT VERSION
==============================================================================

  Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no build step, nothing to
  compile. Edit a file, put it in the repository, and it is live.

  Everything a visitor reads lives in the .html files. The look lives in
  styles.css and pages.css. The behaviour lives in script.js and pages.js.

  Opened straight from a folder the site works, except the search: a browser
  will not let one local file read another, and the search says so plainly.
  Everything else can be checked by double-clicking a file.


==============================================================================
  2  THE PAGES
==============================================================================

  The site has two halves, and both share the same colours and typefaces.

  THE FIRST HALF is the curriculum vitae, with a sidebar down the left:

    index.html                    Home
    academics.html                Academics
    research.html                 Research
    teaching.html                 Teaching
    professional-activities.html  Professional Activities

  These five offer all four appearance controls, including Layout, and the
  sidebar carries a "More to Explore" button that leads to the other half.

  THE SECOND HALF is everything around the CV, with a top bar and no sidebar:

    noticeboard.html              Noticeboard (what has changed lately)
    bio.html                      Biography
    gallery.html                  Photos
    news.html                     News & Updates
    blogs.html                    Blogs (the list)
    useful-links.html             Useful Links
    announcements.html            Important Announcements

  The first six are the six links in the bar. Announcements is built exactly
  like them and carries the same bar, but is deliberately not one of its
  links: it is reached from the Noticeboard, the home page and the Site Guide.

  These pages carry NO tagline under the title. A hairline separates the head
  of the page from the first section instead.

  TWO PAGES CARRY NO BAR AT ALL:

    navigation.html               The Site Guide
    404.html                      Error 404

  They still load pages.js, which is what lets the / key open the search.

  BLOG POSTS live one folder down, in blogs/. They carry no bar either, on
  purpose: a piece should be read without anything else asking for attention.

  AND ONE FILE THAT IS NOT A PAGE:

    google6f4968af0aa3ceef.html   proves the site is yours to Google.
                                  Never edit it, never link to it, never
                                  delete it.


==============================================================================
  3  THE FILES
==============================================================================

  *.html            one file per page. All the text lives here.
  blogs/*.html      one file per blog post.

  styles.css        the whole look of the first half, plus the ten colour
                    schemes, five typefaces, five layouts and dark mode,
                    which the second half uses too.
  pages.css         what the second half does differently: its top bar, its
                    page heads, the blog pages and the two round buttons on
                    a post.

  script.js         shared by every page: the appearance controls, the photo
                    lightbox, the tabs, the "More" blocks, the rotating news
                    cards and the "Last updated" line. LAST_UPDATED is the
                    one line you normally change, at the top.
  pages.js          the top bar, the search, the filter buttons on the Blogs
                    and News pages, and the two buttons on a blog post.
  quotes.js         one quotation for every day of the year, keyed "MM-DD",
                    29 February included. Shown on the Noticeboard.

  gallery/          56 photographs.
  slides/           talk slides and posters, linked from News and from
                    Professional Activities.
  Deep_CV.pdf       the CV. A new one replaces it under the same name.
  photo.jpg         the portrait used on the home page and in link previews.

  sitemap.xml       tells search engines which pages are worth re-reading.
  robots.txt        points them at the sitemap.
  favicon.ico, icon.svg, apple-touch-icon.png    the little tab icons.

  note.txt          this file.


==============================================================================
  4  EVERY TIME YOU CHANGE ANYTHING
==============================================================================

  1. Update LAST_UPDATED at the top of script.js. It writes the date into
     the footer of every page.

  2. Move that page's <lastmod> in sitemap.xml to the same day.

  3. If you added a NEW page, add it to the PAGES list near the top of the
     search block in pages.js, or the search will never read it. Add it to
     the Site Guide (navigation.html) and to 404.html as well.

  4. If you touched the appearance panel, the top bar, or the <head> script
     that restores the reader's colour and theme, make the same change on
     EVERY page. They are repeated in each file on purpose, so that a page
     works on its own.


==============================================================================
  5  HOW TO ADD THE USUAL THINGS
==============================================================================

  A PUBLICATION OR PREPRINT
    research.html, under "List of Publications". Copy the <li> above and
    change it. The list runs newest first and the numbers rise DOWN the page,
    so renumber the ones above it. If the paper has an abstract, give the
    abstract panel a fresh id and point the button at it.

  A NEWS ITEM
    news.html, at the top of the list. Give it data-cats with its tags
    (preprint, publication, talk, conference, position, milestone, life) and
    repeat each tag as a small chip at the end of the line. Update the counts in
    brackets on the filter buttons above the list.
    A big item also belongs on the home page (index.html, the Latest News
    card) and on the Noticeboard.

  AN ANNOUNCEMENT
    announcements.html. A live one goes at the top of "Active
    Announcements". When it has run its course, move it into "Past
    Announcements" and give it a second date: the day it came down.
    Give it data-cats with its tags (for now just "general"; an item may
    carry several) and repeat each tag as a chip at the end of the line, as on
    the News page. The tag buttons sit inside "Past Announcements" only, so
    they filter the past ones and leave the live ones alone; when you add a
    tag or a past item, add its button and update the counts by hand. The
    Noticeboard's copy of a live announcement carries the same chips.
    Commented-out examples sit in the file. Copy one and uncomment it.
    The Noticeboard and the home page each show the latest one.

  A TALK OR A CONFERENCE
    professional-activities.html. Copy the entry above and keep the same
    order of lines. The role markers are the shaded tags: Attended only,
    Attended online, Presented, Organised, chaired a session.

  A COURSE, OR A TEACHING-ASSISTANT ROW
    teaching.html. One row per course.

  A PHOTOGRAPH
    gallery.html, in one of the six sections: Profile Pictures, General
    Photos, Evolution, B.Sc., M.Sc., Ph.D. Journey. Put the file in gallery/
    and keep the file name exactly as it is on disk, including the case of
    the extension (.jpg and .JPG are different files to a web server).
    File names are numbered in tens, so a photograph can be slipped between
    two others without renaming anything.

  A BLOG POST
    Copy a file in blogs/, then copy one <li> in blogs.html and point it at
    the new file. Add the post to the PAGES list in pages.js, and update the
    two counts on the Blogs filter buttons: "All", and the post's own tag.
    Inside a post, <h2> starts a section and <h3> a point inside one. That is
    all the contents button needs. The first letter of the first paragraph is
    drawn large by pages.css (a drop cap); nothing to do by hand.

  A LINK ON THE USEFUL LINKS PAGE
    useful-links.html. Copy a link and its one-line description. The first
    three in a section are shown and the rest are folded away behind "More",
    which appears by itself once there are four.


==============================================================================
  6  HOW THE MOVING PARTS WORK
==============================================================================

  APPEARANCE
    Four controls, in this order: Theme, Colour, Typeface, Layout.
    Theme     Light, Dark, or the same as your device.
    Colour    Terracotta, Mocha, Gold, Sage, Emerald, Teal, Blue, Indigo,
              Plum, Graphite.
    Typeface  Classic, Readable, Sturdy, Bold, Fancy.
    Layout    Classic, Midnight, Parchment, Sticker, Cards. FIRST HALF ONLY:
              the five layouts are built around a sidebar.
    The choice is kept in the browser under site-mode, site-theme, site-font
    and site-style, and a short script in every page's <head> puts it back
    before the page paints. The stored words are not the words on screen:
    dusk is Blue, grove is Parchment, contrast is Sticker, card is Cards,
    cream is Terracotta.

  SEARCH
    On every page. The round button fixed in the top right corner of a
    first-half page on a laptop (drawn like the button on a blog post, so
    change one, change the other), the magnifier in the top bar on a phone
    or tablet (960px and below), and the / key anywhere.
    There is NO index file to rebuild: the first time the search is used it
    fetches the pages themselves, which takes a moment and then is instant.
    A result shows where it came from (page, then section, then sub-section)
    and jumps to that exact line with the word lit up.
    NINE FILTERS sit under the box. They are fixed and written out once, in
    SCOPES at the top of the search block in pages.js: Only this page,
    Publications & Preprints, Teaching, Talks, Conferences, News, Blogs,
    Content of Blogs, Everything but the blog.
    They open FOLDED to one line: as many as fit on the first line, then a
    small round button (›) that shows the rest and turns into ‹ to fold them
    again. How many fit is measured, so it changes with the screen width. The
    row folds again every time the search is opened. Folding never switches
    a filter off: when a switched-on filter is folded away, the round button
    is lit like a switched-on filter to say so. If all nine fit on one line,
    the button does not appear. The first one is
    not a fixed page but whichever page is being read. Two filters at once
    mean either. A filter chosen with an empty box simply lists what is in it.
    A heading can answer to other names: data-also="contact, email, address"
    on a heading is why "contact" finds "Get in Touch". Those names are
    searched and never shown. About 110 are in place.
    Accents, full stops and hyphens are ignored on both sides, so "poincare"
    finds "Poincaré" and "phd" finds "Ph.D.".
    The quotation of the day is put on the Noticeboard by quotes.js after the
    page loads, so the search cannot see it. That is expected.

  THE TAG BUTTONS on the Blogs, News and Important Announcements pages
    Each item carries data-cats, each button carries data-filter, and the
    counts in brackets are typed in by hand. Block 7 of pages.js does the
    filtering. A row of buttons works only on the items in its own section
    (on Blogs and News that is the whole page; on Announcements, the past
    ones, which are all shown: that list has no More button).
    Once a row needs more than two lines it folds to two, with a round
    button (›) at the end of the second line that shows the rest and turns
    into ‹. The button is lit when the tag switched on is folded away.
    The Noticeboard's Latest News lines carry the same chips as News.

  THE TWO BUTTONS ON A BLOG POST
    Bottom right, in a small capsule: back to the top on the left, the
    contents on the right. The left one appears only once the reader is
    500px down. The right one is always there and opens a panel listing the
    piece's own headings, built from them every time the page opens.
    Both are the top bar's own button and menu components, so they match the
    profile and appearance menus in every colour and in dark mode.

  THE QUOTE OF THE DAY
    Opens the Noticeboard. The words live in quotes.js, one line per day.

  THE PHOTO LIGHTBOX
    Click a photograph to open it large; arrow keys move, Escape closes.

  THE "MORE" BLOCKS
    Useful Links only. script.js adds the button by
    itself once a list is long enough, and flips the word between More and
    Less.


==============================================================================
  7  HOUSE RULES: WORDING, DATES, NAMES
==============================================================================

  DATES are written the same way everywhere a visitor sees one:
      25 Jul 2026     day, short month, year.
  A date not yet known is written [dd Mmm yyyy], which cannot be mistaken for
  a real one. Where only the month is known, the month and year alone.

  THE PAGE NAMES a visitor reads are: Home, Academics, Research, Teaching,
  Professional Activities, Noticeboard, Biography, Photos, News & Updates,
  Blogs, Useful Links, Important Announcements, The Site Guide.
  One file name does not match its page: navigation.html is the Site Guide.
  The file keeps its old name so that no link anyone has saved ever breaks.

  THE FOOT of every page carries one line: "Can't find what you're looking
  for? Try the Site Guide." The Site Guide itself is the only page without it.

  THE EMAIL ADDRESS appears on the home page and in the Site Guide's "Found a
  bug?" panel. Change one and change the other.

  CLASS NAMES in the second half all begin pg- or tb-. The appearance panel
  is the exception: it borrows the first half's own controls so that the two
  halves cannot drift apart.

  ON A PHONE (860px and below) the first half's pages move their title into
  the top bar as the page scrolls, and the second half's pages put the page
  name in place of the site name.

==============================================================================
 10  DECIDED AND CLOSED
==============================================================================

  Do not reopen these without a reason:

  * The alternate home page was cancelled and deleted.
  * The "Background Image" appearance option was designed and dropped.
  * The five layouts and the ten colours are settled. Do not restyle them.
  * The blog posts carry no top bar, on purpose.
  * Tag-based search filtering (the old "#" mechanism) was replaced by the
    nine fixed filters. Do not bring back data-tags.


==============================================================================
 11  CHECKS WORTH RUNNING
==============================================================================

  After any change:
    * Open the page and read it. Check every link you touched.
    * Check it at a phone's width as well as a laptop's.

  After a big change:
    * All five layouts and both light and dark, on a first-half page and a
      second-half page.
    * Text contrast of at least 4.5:1 in every colour scheme. Measure it
      properly: the colours are mixed with color-mix(), so a value read from
      the stylesheet is not the colour on screen.
    * The search: open it, type two words, pick a result, and check it lands
      on the right line with the word lit up.
    * On a blog post: scroll down, check the arrow appears, open the contents
      and jump to a section.
    * Tag balance, duplicate ids and broken links across every page.
